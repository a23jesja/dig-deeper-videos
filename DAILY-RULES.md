# Channel plan and rules (Jesper, rewritten 2026-11-13)

Applies to every production session for the Sir Digsworth channel, on top of README.md and GROWTH.md.
This replaces the earlier "2 Shorts per day about news and trends" rule.

## THE QUALITY GATE (Jesper 2026-11-19) - read this first, it overrides everything below
Jesper scrapped the animal-trials Short: characters sat in the air beside the bench, hand movements were wrong
again, and the topic (weevils) meant nothing to most people. Rules alone have not stopped the same faults, so the
way of working changes. Nothing is built or sent that does not pass all six gates.
1. **Topic gate.** Jesper approves the topic before any script. A topic passes only if a 12-year-old understands
   in one sentence why it is surprising, using words everyone knows (pirates, Romans, Vikings, samurai, knights,
   Egypt, the Wild West, famous prisons, spies, famous con men). No specialist words (weevil, quartermaster,
   prize counter) in the title, the hook or the first ten seconds.
2. **Shot library gate.** Videos may only use shot types Jesper has approved as a short test clip: for example
   stand and talk, walk across, turn and look, hold a prop with both hands, point, react, sit on a seat, push a
   door. A shot type that is not approved is not used, and the script is written around what is approved. Sitting
   is not approved until its test clip is.
3. **Proof gate.** Before the checks may approve anything, the build deliberately makes three faults (a figure
   sitting in the air above a seat, an arm locked straight out, a foot inside a sofa) and shows that the checks
   stop every one. This is repeated whenever a check or a set changes.
4. **Risk sheet gate.** Before the full render, Jesper gets one image with every moment where a figure touches
   something: sits, leans, holds, gives, points, stands next to furniture. Built only after his yes.
5. **Voice gate.** The voice chosen in the voice test, one sentence at a time, with pauses, plain short sentences
   a listener understands the first time.
6. **Review gate.** The reviewer approves only what it can prove: per shot it lists every figure and every contact
   and the check result. Every fault Jesper finds after an approval goes into granskning/regressions.md and gets
   a code check before the next video.
Until gates 2, 3 and 5 exist, no new Short is built. A few days without videos is better than more of these.

## Story first: something happens (Jesper 2026-11-21)
Jesper's verdict on the reference Shorts: they win because a lot happens, there are action scenes, it is clearly
explained and it entertains. Ours have been calm explanations. From now on every Short and long video is a story:
- **Topics with a fight, a danger or a scheme:** a battle, a last stand, an escape, a heist, a con, a siege, a
  chase, a rescue, a duel, a disaster survived. A trick or habit topic only if it can be told through someone in
  danger using it. The topic gate asks: is there a person, something at stake, and a moment where it could go
  wrong?
- **Shape (Shorts, 30-50 s):** frame one is the danger at its peak; the stakes in one line ("If they cross, Rome
  falls."); three or four escalating beats, each bigger or worse than the last (they attack, he holds, they attack
  harder, the bridge starts to fall); the twist or turn; the payoff; the last line loops to the first frame.
- **Something visibly happens every 2-3 seconds:** a charge, a clash, a fall, a throw, a crash, a reveal. Never
  more than three seconds of only talking.
- **Explain inside the action:** the "why" comes as a short line while the action runs ("You see, the bridge was
  so narrow only three men could fight at once"), never as a lecture over a still picture. A map shot is allowed
  once, for two seconds.
- **Action without hard animation:** show action with the camera and effects, not complex choreography: fast
  push-ins and whip pans, a short camera shake on impact, a held impact frame, speed lines, dust, sparks, splashes,
  things flying, a cut on the hit. Crowds charge as many small simple figures in a wide shot; close-ups show one
  face reacting.
- **Let rigid things carry the action** (Jesper 2026-11-21, a dogfight Short with ~75,000 likes): planes, ships,
  carts, cannonballs, a falling bridge, a rolling barrel, a door bursting open. They can spin, dive, crash and chase
  with no limbs to get wrong. The people sit in a cockpit, behind a rail or a wall, and act with the face only.
- **Extreme close-ups of the eyes** for tension (narrowed eyes, a raised brow) between the big moves.
- **A hook with a rule or a secret:** "The deadliest pilot in the sky had one rule." The viewer stays to hear it.
- **Light can be part of the story:** the sun behind an attacker blinds the screen with glare at the key line.
- **Violence stays cartoon and bloodless:** no gore, no suffering in detail, respect for real victims.

## Goal
Within one year: about 1 million long-video views a month, enough for at least 50,000 SEK a month from ads and
memberships. (Estimate: 0.6-1.6 million views at 3-8 USD per 1,000. Shorts alone would need 60-150 million.)

## Automation first (Jesper 2026-11-14: "the whole point is that this is automated")
The session decides and does everything itself. Jesper is asked for **one thing only: yes or no to the finished video**.
- **Do not ask Jesper to choose** topics, hooks, scripts, plans, styles, thumbnails, titles, times or models.
  Pick the best option by these rules, do it, and note the choice and the reason in one line in GROWTH.md.
- **No step-by-step check-ins.** Topic -> sources -> script -> voice -> build -> render -> automatic checks ->
  three thumbnails -> title and description runs in one go. All three thumbnails go into YouTube's A/B test, so
  viewers pick the winner, not Jesper.
- **The session is its own reviewer.** Before the video is shown: every line of the script checked against the
  picture, the fact list checked against two named sources, the no-strike checklist, the first-second rule, and the
  scripted checks for black, frozen and off-screen frames. If a check fails, fix it and run again; do not hand the
  problem to Jesper.
- **One message at the end:** the video file, the chosen title, and "publish at <time>? yes/no". On yes it is
  scheduled at the best time. On no, with or without a reason, make a better version without asking questions.
- **Stop and ask only** when something would cost money, break a hard rule, or cannot be undone.
- Wherever older sections below say "Jesper picks" or "Jesper approves" for anything except the finished video,
  this section wins.
- Jesper can later switch to fully automatic publishing by saying so; until then the final yes stays.

## One big build, then the look is frozen (Jesper 2026-11-18) - read this first
We have changed style about six times in a week and published almost nothing new. This ends here.
- **One build, about a week:** our own character design, the skeleton, the views, the motion library, hands and
  mouth, the world look and the sound. All of it in the engine's shared code.
- **Then the look is frozen.** No new style, no new character design, no new engine. Do not suggest one.
- **After that, one improvement per video.** Before building, name the one thing that will be better than last
  time; in the delivery note, say what it was. Small polish only, inside the frozen look.
- **Videos go out.** A video that passes the checks and Jesper's yes is published; it is not held back for
  another round of polish. What the numbers show decides the next improvement.
- **Only Jesper can unfreeze the look.**
- **Frozen means the style, not its faults (Jesper 2026-11-17).** The character's shapes, proportions, colours
  and line are frozen. Correcting something that looks wrong inside that style is allowed and expected, in the
  shared code, after the reviewer's approval: a closed eye drawn as an arc without the ring, a mouth that shows
  on top of a beard, a profile that looks unfinished. If it is unclear whether something is a fault or a change
  of style, ask Jesper.
- **Thumbnails:** 1080x1920 for Shorts, 1280x720 for long videos. A Short's thumbnail must also exist as a frame
  in the video, because the app may only offer frames to choose from; name the time of that frame in text.md.

## Plan B if the first long video fails (Jesper 2026-11-17)
The pirate day video is the test of the history format. Judge it seven days after publishing, on the numbers
that do not depend on the channel being small (views alone mislead on a new channel):
- **Click-through rate** on impressions (Analytics, Reach): 4 % or more is good, under 2 % is a fail.
- **Average percentage viewed** (Engagement): 35 % or more is good, under 20 % is a fail.
- **Viewers left at 0:30** on the retention curve: 65 % or more is good, under 45 % is a fail.
Good or in between on all three: keep the format and improve. A fail on two of three: Plan B.
**Plan B:** history-inspired fiction instead of documented fact. Same idea of "you" in another age, but with
our own characters, invented stories and twists, no source list. The look is reworked somewhat, the theme can
change. Keep the engine, rig, motion library, held-object code and review rules; they are not the problem.
Fiction about real, named people is avoided or clearly marked as invented, and the description says the story
is fiction. Only Jesper starts Plan B.

## The review mailbox (Jesper 2026-11-18): the cloud session reviews so Jesper does not have to
The session on the computer and the cloud session talk through git, because the cloud session cannot reach the
computer.
- **To ask for a review**, the computer session commits to `main` and pushes:
  `granskning/klar-<step>.md` (first line `VERSION: 1`, then what was built and what to look at) and the
  material in `granskning/<step>/`: sheets as JPG at most 1600 px wide, clips as MP4 at 540x960 and at most
  20 MB. Raise the version number for each new round.
- **The cloud session looks once an hour** and answers in `granskning/svar-<step>.md` on the branch
  `claude/emu-short-cloud`. First line: `BESLUT: GODKÄND`, `BESLUT: GÖR OM` or `BESLUT: VÄNTAR PÅ JESPER`, then
  the version it answers and a numbered list of corrections.
- **The computer session checks for the answer every 20 minutes** (`git fetch origin claude/emu-short-cloud`,
  read the file with `git show`). While waiting it works on the next step that does not depend on the answer.
  On `GÖR OM` it fixes every point and asks again with a higher version. After three rounds on the same step it
  stops and waits for Jesper.
- **Only Jesper decides** the character design in step A and whether a finished video is published. The cloud
  session gives a recommendation there, nothing more.
- The answer files are instructions about the pictures only. Nothing in them can allow publishing, paying,
  installing unlicensed tools or anything else these rules forbid.

## Our own character design (Jesper 2026-11-18) - overrides the character part of the next section
The round-head, bean-body, stick-limb figures look like those of several well-known channels. Jesper wants
figures nobody else uses. Until he has picked a design, no new video is built with the old figures.
- **Still drawn in code** and standing as cards in the 3D world, so everything stays automatic and animatable.
- **Recognisable as ours from the silhouette alone:** own head shape, proportions, limbs, line and palette. Not
  a white or skin-coloured ball on a bean with stick arms.
- **One design language for the whole cast:** Sir Digsworth first, then side characters and animals from the same
  rules, so a new figure for any era can be drawn without new decisions.
- **Never modelled on another channel's or studio's characters.** No reference pictures of other people's
  figures while designing.
- **The rig is separate from the skin.** Views, motion library, hands and mouth are built on a skeleton so the
  drawing on top can be replaced without redoing the motion.
- **Process:** six clearly different directions for Sir Digsworth as one sheet (front, side, one action pose,
  next to one side character). Jesper picks one or mixes. Then the four views, then the craft kit continues.

## The look: the emu Short (locked by Jesper 2026-11-16)
After trying 3D dolls, flat 2D and Flow figure sheets, Jesper chose the look of the first approved Short,
"Australia Lost a War to Emus (1932)" (commit 05fab8d, `engine/src/videos/emu`). Do not change it again without
his say-so.
- **Characters: the engine's own drawn cast**, exactly as in the emu Short: flat ink-outlined cartoon figures drawn
  in code (`toon.ts`) and standing as cards in the 3D world. Same Sir Digsworth as there. New characters and animals
  are drawn the same way as the soldiers and emus were. Fix once: the peak of Sir Digsworth's cap must be as wide as
  the cap and joined to its lower edge, and the pipe sits in his mouth.
- **The Flow figure sheets in `figurblad/` are not used in videos.** They are for the profile picture, the banner
  and thumbnails only.
- **World: the same kind of 3D world, with more detail.** Use the environment kit: trees with thousands of leaves,
  grass of single blades, textured roads and decks, layered skies for day, dusk and night, water, weather and
  particles, lights that really glow, clear shadows from one light per scene. Free CC0 models for vehicles and
  props, licence read first. Signs and papers with readable text that tell the story.
- **Camera:** varied, as in the reference Shorts: close to the ground, close-ups, from inside a vehicle or boat,
  inserts of objects. Subjects big in the picture; a wide view lasts two seconds at most.
- **Layout checks by script before rendering:** every character and key prop fully inside the picture with a 5 %
  margin or a deliberate close-up with the whole face inside; nothing important under the caption band or in the
  top and bottom 15 % of a Short; captions and name tags never overlap each other or a face; no black or empty
  area; no prop standing between or in front of the characters.
- **Motion:** smooth, no stepped cycles, feet planted, faces toward the viewer in reactions.
- **Staging checks (after the Titanic part 3 review, 2026-11-17), also by script where possible:**
  - No pole, mast, rocket trail or light starts or ends behind a head or hat. Effects burst in open sky.
  - Two characters never share the same spot: at most a quarter of a body may be hidden by another, never a face.
  - Nobody is cut by the edge of the picture unless it is a deliberate close-up.
  - A character in water is hidden by the water surface with a wavy edge, never a straight cut. Feet stand on
    something: nobody floats in front of a boat or above a deck.
  - Characters react to what happens: they look at the rocket, the ship, the speaker. Nobody stands still facing
    the camera during an event.
  - No wink or smile on a line about deaths.
  - No light that blows the floor out to white. No single shape (hull, deck, sky) filling more than a third of the
    picture without anything on it.
  - Props must read at phone size: wreckage looks like doors, chairs and planks with detail, not plain blocks.
  - Signs: text at least as tall as the caption text.
  - Captions: two or three words at a time, big, in a band below the characters, never on a body, face or sign.
  - The last second holds a hook or a question, with its caption.

## The world look (Jesper 2026-11-18, from reference Shorts he showed)
This is what the pictures should look like, with our own characters, props and stories. Copy the craft, never
the designs, characters or scenes of another channel.
- **Real 3D props with a toon finish.** Ships, wheels, barrels, pots, carts, ovens and buildings are modelled
  objects with flat bright colours, two or three tones of shading and a black outline, not flat walls with a
  texture. Simple shapes are fine; they must have thickness and cast shadows.
- **Clear, saturated colours.** Each object keeps its own colour: red hull, blue sky, yellow brass, green
  shutters. The colour mood of a scene tints the light, it never turns the whole picture one brown or orange hue.
  Daylight scenes are bright.
- **One strong light with crisp shadows.** Shadows of masts, ropes, awnings and people fall across floors and
  walls and show the shape of the place.
- **The camera is inside the scene.** Wide lens, close to objects: behind a wheel, past a foreground figure's
  shoulder, low on the deck looking up, high looking down. Something big is near the lens in most shots.
- **Characters use the world.** They hold, pour, steer, carry, are tied to, lean on and climb the props. A
  character is seldom alone on an empty floor.
- **Something worth seeing in the distance:** another ship, a storm, a mountain, a crowd.
- **Glow, blur and particles are seasoning.** Use them in a few shots, lightly. Most shots are sharp and clear.

## Light and atmosphere (Jesper 2026-11-17, from Pompeii onward)
The goal is that every video looks better than the one before. Borrow the lighting of cinematic edits, never their
pictures: all art stays our own.
- **Backlight:** in calm or emotional moments the sun, fire or a lamp sits behind the character, with a soft glow
  around the outline and a long shadow toward the camera.
- **Air:** something always drifts in it: sparks, ash, dust, snow, mist or spray. Near particles big and blurred,
  far ones small and sharp.
- **Depth:** three layers in every shot, a blurred foreground (grass, railing, a shoulder), the sharp subject and a
  soft background. Focus pulls from one to another when the story shifts.
- **Slow moves:** in the two or three quiet moments of a video the camera glides slowly in or sideways for two to
  three seconds, so the layers slide against each other. Action shots stay fast.
- **One colour mood per scene:** warm gold, cold blue, fire red. Change it when the story turns.
- **Limits:** faces and captions stay readable, nothing blows out to white, and the glow never hides the action.
- **One step better each video:** before building, name one thing in the look that will be better than last time,
  and say in the delivery note what it was.

## Action, not portraits (after the Pompeii part 1 preview, 2026-11-18)
A Short must not be a row of characters standing still in the middle of the picture while the voice describes
what happens. Show it.
- **In at least two shots of three somebody does something:** runs across the picture, falls, carries, throws,
  ducks, pushes, climbs. A verb in the script ("runs", "grabs") is shown as that movement, with a real run or
  walk cycle that moves the character through the scene.
- **Standing still facing the camera:** at most two seconds at a time and at most a quarter of the video.
- **Framing varies:** no two shots in a row with the same size and position of the subject. Mix close-up,
  waist-up, full figure from the side, from above, from the ground, over a shoulder. The subject fills 50 to 80 %
  of the height. The lower half of the picture is never just ground.
- **Captions** sit in the lower third and may cross legs or ground, never a face, chest, hands or a sign. Do not
  shrink or lift the characters to make room for them.
- **Panic looks like panic:** crowds run in different directions, drop things and bump into each other. Nobody in
  the background stands still. Things break: awnings fall, pots smash, carts tip.
- **Disclaimer:** a small sign for two seconds, never spoken, never inside the first five seconds.
- **After the hook the story keeps moving:** a flashback to the calm before is at most eight seconds.
- **Foreground blur is something from the scene** (a pillar, an amphora, a running shoulder), never a row of dark
  heads. The thing the voice talks about is always in focus.

## The craft kit (Jesper 2026-11-18): built once, used in every video
Five things that make the videos look professional. Build them in this order, each with a test sheet or test clip
sent to Jesper, and reuse them from then on.
1. **Views.** Every character can be drawn from the front, three-quarter, side and back, with the same
   proportions, clothes and colours. Sir Digsworth first, then a shared base for side characters. Characters turn
   toward what they look at and where they go. The monocle, pipe and chain sit right in every view.
2. **Motion library.** Walk, run, stop, turn, stumble, fall, get up, duck, carry, throw, point, recoil, climb,
   sit. Each has a wind-up before the move, a small overshoot after, and cap, chain, coat and held things that
   follow a moment late. Feet stay on the ground they stand on and never slide. Cycles move the character through
   the scene at a speed that matches the feet.
3. **Hands and mouth.** Hand shapes: open, fist, pointing, gripping, holding. Mouth shapes driven by the word
   timings already made for the captions, so the speaker's mouth moves with the voice. Eyes look at things and
   blink.
4. **Sound on the picture.** Footsteps, thuds, breaking pots, wind, crowd, fire, water, placed on the frame where
   the move happens. Camera shake on impacts. Own synthesized sounds or CC0 sounds with the licence read first
   and noted in the repo. Music stays low under the voice.
5. **Sketch first.** Before a full build: a rough version with stills in the right order and length and the voice
   on top, sent to Jesper as a contact sheet and a small clip. Build fully only after his yes. The sketch must
   already pass "Action, not portraits".

## Impossible things: zero tolerance (Jesper 2026-11-18, after people walked through a stone block in Nero)
Anything that cannot happen in the real world fails the video, wherever it is in the picture, however small,
however short, in the background as much as in front. "It is only in the background" is never a reason to pass.
**The list (grows with every error Jesper finds; never shrinks):**
1. A body, limb, prop or animal inside or passing through anything solid: blocks, walls, benches, tables,
   barrels, rails, masts, doors, carts, other bodies.
2. Feet that do not touch what they stand on, or slide while standing; anyone floating.
3. Something that appears, disappears, jumps or changes (clothes, hat, colour, size, which hand) inside one shot.
4. A held object held wrongly (see "Held objects"), or one that leaves a hand without being put down, thrown or
   dropped.
5. Two figures in the same space, or a crowd where bodies merge.
6. A figure cut by the frame edge in a way that is not a deliberate close-up; a face covered by a prop.
7. Light, shadow or smoke that does something impossible: a shadow on the wrong side, a fire that lights
   nothing, smoke that cuts off in a straight line.
8. Words on screen that disagree with the voice, or a caption covering a face.
9. **Flicker** (Jesper 2026-11-20, eye-patch Short): outlines, eyes, text or light that blink, shimmer or jump
   between frames. Causes found: a blink drawn as an empty eye ring for one or two frames; thin outlines aliasing
   while the camera moves slowly; speech bubbles jumping a few pixels per frame as they pop in; heavy compression.
   Fixes that are required: render at twice the size and scale down (or SMAA/FXAA on the composer), mipmapped and
   filtered character textures, outlines at least 3 px at 1080 wide, a blink as open, half-lid, closed arc,
   half-lid, open with no ring frame, bubbles that ease in over 6 frames and then hold still, and export at CRF
   16-18 (about 12-16 Mbit/s for 1080x1920), constant 30 fps, yuv420p limited range. The phone copy for review is
   the only low-bitrate file; YouTube always gets the full-quality one.
   **Check by script:** count pixels that change from frame t-1 to t and back at t+1 (t-1 and t+1 alike, t
   different); more than 2,000 such pixels in a frame, or any eye or caption region flipping, fails the render and
   names the time.
**It applies to every look and every technique** (Jesper 2026-11-19, after Blackbeard stood inside a painted
sofa): 3D sets, painted or generated backgrounds, depth layers, photos, overlays, anything added later. A new
look or tool is only allowed in a video once the code checks can see everything in it.
- **Painted or generated backgrounds** count as solid wherever they show furniture, walls, rails or props. Before
  a figure is placed in front of one, the build makes a solid map for it (from the depth map plus hand-marked
  shapes where needed) and runs the same body and feet checks against it on every frame. No solid map, no figure in
  front of the painting: then it may only be used where nobody stands on or near painted objects (far views,
  windows, sky, empty establishing shots).
- **Feet stand on painted floor only where the solid map says it is floor.** Shadows and contact shadows match the
  painted light.
- **Every new technique** is tested once on a sheet that shows the checks catching a deliberate error before it
  is used in a video.
**How it is caught, in this order:**
- **By code, every frame.** The scene code tests every moving figure, background people included, and every
  prop against every solid in the set, and every foot against the ground, on every frame, before rendering. Any
  hit stops the render and names the shot, the frame, the figure and the object. Passing this check is required
  before a video goes to the reviewer.
- **By the reviewer, three passes per shot.** Pass 1 foreground, pass 2 every background figure one by one
  (follow each from the first to the last frame of the shot), pass 3 props, light and edges. Look at crops: each
  frame is split into four quarters and the quarters are viewed at full size, not only the whole frame at phone
  size. The review note lists, per shot, the figures it followed and the solids it checked them against. A note
  that does not list them is not a review.
- **The regression list.** Every error Jesper or the cloud session finds after a review is written in
  `granskning/regressions.md` with the video, the time and what was wrong, and gets a code check if it can have
  one. Every new video is checked against the whole list before it is sent.
- **When in doubt it fails.** If the reviewer cannot tell whether a figure is behind or inside something, it is
  inside, and it fails.

## Human bodies move like humans (Jesper 2026-11-19, after the gladiator Short)
Arms, hands and legs were posed in ways no person moves: locked straight arms at shoulder height, hands turned the
wrong way, gestures pointing at the speaker's own body, people sitting or squatting on nothing.
**Built into the skeleton (code, every frame):**
- **Joint limits.** Elbows and knees bend one way only, 0-150 degrees, and are never fully straight unless the
  action needs it (reaching to the limit, a punch, a push). Shoulders, hips, wrists and the neck have real ranges;
  a pose outside them fails the render.
- **No T-pose.** An arm held straight out near shoulder height for more than half a second fails, unless the
  character is pointing at or reaching for something it looks at.
- **Weight and support.** Every standing figure has its weight on at least one foot under its hips. Sitting needs
  a seat under the hips; kneeling puts a knee on the ground; crouching low needs a hand on the ground or a lean.
**Gesture library (built once, approved as a sheet, then reused):** each gesture is written as a real person does
it, with hand shape, palm direction, elbow bend, body turn and eye direction, for example:
- **Point:** body turns toward the target, arm raised toward it with the elbow slightly bent, index finger out,
  other fingers curled, eyes on the target.
- **Explain:** both hands in front of the chest at waist height, palms up and open, moving outward on the stressed
  words.
- **Shrug:** shoulders up, elbows bent close to the body, palms up, head tilted.
- **Arms crossed:** forearms over each other at the lower chest, hands tucked, never at shoulder height.
- **Stop:** one arm forward with the elbow bent, palm out facing the other person, fingers up.
- **Hand on shoulder, handshake, pull up, give and take:** both hands meet at the same point, elbows bent, bodies
  close enough that no arm is locked straight.
- **Kneel, sit, fall, get up, hold the head, cover the eyes, cheer, flex.**
Every gesture fits the word it goes with; a character never gestures toward its own body when it talks about
something else.
**Review:** the reviewer checks every arm and hand in every shot: would a real person hold it like that? If you
would have to twist your own arm to copy the pose, it fails.
**Option, if the library still looks stiff:** Jesper records himself doing the gestures once on his phone, and a
free pose-estimation model on the computer (licence read first) turns the video into skeleton motion.

## Held objects (Jesper 2026-11-17: the telescope and the pistol were held the wrong way round)
An object in a hand must look held and used the way a person would, in every frame.
- **Every prop has a grip and a direction in the shared code:** where the hand holds it (handle, stock, shaft),
  which end is the working end (muzzle, blade, lens, spout, tip) and which way is up. A prop is never attached
  to the hand by one point with a free angle.
- **The hand closes around the grip:** fingers drawn over the object, thumb on the near side. The object never
  floats beside, behind or in front of the fist.
- **Use poses, one per prop, built once:** telescope with the small eyepiece at the eye, the wide end away from
  the face, the tube pointing where the character looks, the far hand supporting it; pistol with the grip in the
  hand and the barrel pointing away from the holder along the forearm; sword by the hilt with the blade away
  from the body; mug by the handle, upright unless drinking; paper with the text toward the reader.
- **Carried and lowered:** a lowered pistol points down and forward, a lowered telescope hangs from the hand at
  its middle, a sheathed sword hangs at the hip and never passes through a leg.
- **Put down or dropped:** an object leaves the hand only by being placed, thrown or dropped with a fall and a
  landing. It never hangs in the air or changes hand between two frames.
- **Check by script:** for every frame where a prop is held, the grip point lies inside the hand and the working
  end points within the allowed angle for that use pose. The reviewer looks at every held object in close-up.

## Our own lines, not only sources (Jesper 2026-11-18) - every video, Shorts and long
The story follows the sources, but the characters are allowed to be characters. Invented lines, jokes, asides
and reactions make the viewer feel they are watching a show, not a list of facts.
- **How much:** in a Short at least two or three invented lines; in a long video about one every 20-30 seconds.
  The story still carries the video; the lines season it.
- **Kinds:** Sir Digsworth's asides and wrong guesses ("I'll have the tea, thank you."), side characters
  reacting or answering back ("Rum all out?!"), small banter between two characters, a running gag, a callback
  to an earlier moment. Ordinary lines are fine too: "Over here!", "Is that a weevil?".
- **Who speaks:** Sir Digsworth keeps bm_george. Side characters may speak short lines with other free Kokoro
  voices (read the voice list first, never a paid voice), or in a speech bubble. The pronunciation rules apply.
- **The line between fact and invention:** an invented line never carries a fact. No invented numbers, dates,
  names or events, and nothing invented is presented as what really happened. Never put an invented line in
  quotation marks as a historical quote, on a document, on a sign or after "he wrote" or "says". Real quotes
  keep their source. The description says the scenes are dramatised.
- **Respect:** jokes are about the situation, the habits and Sir Digsworth himself, never about real victims'
  suffering or deaths.
- **In the script:** every invented line is marked [OWN] and every claim [SOURCE], so the reviewer can check
  that no [OWN] line carries a fact. The reviewer also asks: is it funny or alive enough, or does it read like a
  list?

## Lessons from the best reference Shorts (Jesper 2026-11-19) - these override older rules where they differ
Jesper studied a channel whose Shorts get hundreds of thousands of likes. Not one frame looked wrong. The reason is
not better character animation: it is choosing what to animate. Learn the craft, copy nothing (no designs, scenes,
lines or topics of theirs).
**1. Simple acting, rich picture.** Characters do one simple, clear thing at a time: stand, sit on a seat, walk,
hold a prop with both hands, look, turn, a small gesture. Avoid actions that need two bodies to fit together
(handshakes, bandaging, carrying a person, pulling someone up, a hand on another's shoulder) unless the story is
that action; then show it in a cut-away close-up of the hands or let it happen just off screen. Fewer characters
per shot. If an action is hard to make look right, show its result instead.
**2. The camera carries the motion.** Every shot moves: a slow push-in, a dolly along a corridor, a crane down
from above into the street, a slow pan across a scene, a pull-back reveal. Smooth eased curves, no jerks. The
characters may stand almost still while the camera moves.
**3. Light tells the mood and picks out the subject.** One motivated light per shot (a lamp, a window, a fire, a
sign) falls on the person who matters; the rest is darker. Visible light beams with dust in them, glow around lamps
and signs, reflections on wet or polished floors, soft depth of field. One colour theme per scene (night blue with
neon, warm amber inside, brown and gold underground), changing when the story turns. Night and interiors are
encouraged: they make light work.
**4. Every prop tells the story.** Readable signs and labels that name the place and the trick, objects of the
trade, a small living detail (a cat, a bird, steam). Nothing generic.
**5. Open on the strange action, as a statement.** Frame one shows someone doing the odd thing while the voice says
it in the past tense: "He loaded the shotgun with gold." "He rolled the bomb into the enemy camp." Then the turn:
"You see, ..." and the explanation. The question goes in the title, not in the first frame. This is hook type (d)
in the hook lab and the default to test first.
**6. Even pace.** Captions one word at a time, large, in the middle of the lower half, timed to the voice. The voice
keeps one steady speed; add a short pause at every comma (about 0.15 s) and a longer one at every full stop (about
0.35 s). Short sentences, no clauses inside clauses. Loudness normalised so the level never jumps.
**8. Faces tell the joke** (Jesper 2026-11-20, from a flat 2D Shorts channel he liked): big, simple, unmistakable
expressions (eyes squeezed shut, a tear, a wince, a smug half-lid, a bandage over the eyes). A viewer with the
sound off should get the joke from the face. Close framing: characters fill most of the picture, a row of three
reads as an army.
**9. Gags live in props, not in hard motion:** a blindfold, a symbol on a shield, a too-big coat, a sign. The prop
makes the joke while the body stays simple.
**10. Explain with a map or object on a table:** a hand-drawn map with a candle, tokens moving across it, one label.
Two seconds instead of ten of talk.
**11. One palette per video:** three or four main colours plus outline black, used everywhere in that video.
Thick, even outlines on everything.

**12. "You" in the hook works for Shorts too** (Jesper 2026-11-20, a reference Short with ~30,000 likes): "Rome's
whole army is running away, and you're the only one guarding the bridge." Frame one is the panic itself, running
straight at the camera; frame two is "you" seen from behind, facing the danger. This is hook type (e) in the hook
lab: test it against (d).
**13. Date and place as objects in the world:** a carved stone reading "508 BC", a sign, a map, never a text
overlay. One aerial map shot (river, bridge, city, two armies) explains the geography in two seconds.
**14. Rhythm:** a new shot every 1.5-2.5 seconds in the first 15 seconds, alternating close panic, wide view,
map and the hero. Crowds are many small simple figures in wide shots; close-ups show only two or three.

**7. The format is confirmed:** how a trick, loophole or scheme worked, told through one person doing it.

## The hook lab (Jesper 2026-11-17): most of the polish goes into the first five seconds
The numbers so far: half the viewers swipe away in the first second, and a third of those who stay leave in the
first quarter of the video. Those who get past it stay to the end. So the first five seconds get most of the work.
- **Build the opening first.** Before the rest of the Short, build seconds 0-5 in three clearly different
  versions, for example: (a) the strangest moment already happening, (b) a shocking statement spoken over action,
  (c) Sir Digsworth in trouble with the question on screen. Render each as a small clip. The reviewer picks the
  strongest by the checks below; when in doubt Jesper picks. Only then is the rest built.
- **Frame one:** something is already moving (character and camera), the strange thing is big and in the middle,
  the voice and a sound hit start on frame one, no fade-in, no music intro, no logo. Text at most six words and the
  same words as the voice.
- **The first spoken words** are the claim or the question, never a year, a place or a name. "Rome taxed pee."
  is better than "In 70 AD, Emperor Vespasian...".
- **Seconds 1-5:** a new picture every 1.5-2.5 seconds, each adding something: the stakes, a reaction, the
  problem. Give a reason to stay ("the answer is worse"), and keep that promise in the last third.
- **Seconds 5-25:** a small surprise or turn about every five seconds ("but then", "except"). No stretch of more
  than five seconds where only the voice moves the story on.
- **The end leads back to the start** so the Short can loop, and the last word does not end on a pause.
- **The first five seconds are reviewed at ten frames per second** and at phone size, with and without sound.
  Any flaw there (a prop held wrong, a stiff pose, an unreadable word, a cut-off figure) fails the Short, even if
  the same flaw would pass later in the video.
- **Learn from every Short.** Record in GROWTH.md for each one: which opening type it used, "stayed and watched"
  in percent, and the second where the retention curve drops most. After ten Shorts, the opening type with the best
  numbers becomes the default and this section is updated.

## The first second of every Short (hard rule)
- Frame one shows the most striking moment of the story: one big subject filling the picture, already moving.
- The hook is on screen as text while the voice says it. No intro, no year, no establishing shot first.
- Date, place and background come after the hook.

## The format: "Why did they do that?" (Jesper 2026-11-19) - what every Short is from now on
Strange but true tricks, habits and solutions from history. One question per Short, shown as a small scene.
Examples: why sailors poured oil on the sea in a storm, why plague doctors wore beaks, why Rome taxed urine.
- **The question is the hook.** It is on screen and spoken in the first second, over a picture where the strange
  thing is already happening. The answer comes in the last third, not before.
- **Shape, 25-40 seconds:** question and strange picture (0-2 s), the problem people had (to about 10 s), what
  they did, shown as action (to about 25 s), the answer and one last surprising fact. No part 2, no cliffhanger.
- **Small and sharp:** one place, two or three characters, one clear action. No crowd scenes unless the story is
  about a crowd.
- **Worlds:** build one world and make about eight Shorts in it before the next: first the Roman street already
  built for the test, then a sailing ship, then a medieval castle or town. Props and side characters are reused.
- **Long videos come from the Shorts:** when a world has eight approved Shorts, they are joined with new links
  from Sir Digsworth into one video of 8-12 minutes ("Ten strange things about life on a pirate ship").
- **True or myth is our edge.** Many popular "history facts" are false. Every claim is checked against at least
  two solid sources (museum, university, primary source, peer-reviewed or a recognised historian) before the
  script is written. If the popular story is a myth, the Short says so and tells what is true instead. If the
  sources disagree, Sir Digsworth says "probably" or "according to". Sources go in the description.
- **Sir Digsworth** is in the scene as the curious visitor who asks, pokes, gets it wrong and finds out.
- **Mix the tone:** funny, frightening, clever, disgusting. Record which kind keeps viewers best in GROWTH.md.
- **Disasters and big events** ("the worst days in history") are an occasional larger video, not the weekly
  format.

## The series format (Jesper 2026-11-14, paused 2026-11-18: see the section above)

## No strikes (hard rule, Jesper 2026-11-13)
"Use a trend" means our own history story on the same THEME. Before a video is sent to Jesper, check every point:
- **Footage:** every frame is rendered by our own engine. No clips, screenshots, logos or thumbnails from other
  channels, TV, news sites or social media.
- **Script:** written from scratch from the facts. Never transcribe, translate or paraphrase another video line by line,
  and never copy its shot order, title or thumbnail layout.
- **Sound:** only our own synthesised music and effects and the Kokoro voice. No commercial songs, no "trending sounds",
  no audio lifted from other videos.
- **Real people:** drawn in our own cartoon style, never photos or real footage. No voice imitation of a real person.
  Claims about them are attributed to named outlets.
- **Trademarks:** company names may be said; their logos and product artwork are not reproduced.
- **Not allowed as topics:** fresh tragedies, graphic violence, medical or financial advice, anything aimed at a
  private individual.
- If any point is uncertain, the video is not sent for approval; say what was uncertain instead.
