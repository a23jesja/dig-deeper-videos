## Facts used
- It began in July 1518 in Strasbourg when a woman, Frau Troffea, started dancing in the street. [W] [PDR] [PS] [MF]
- She danced for days. [W: about a week] [PDR: four to six days] [MF: three days] — the script says only "for days".
- Within days more than thirty had joined. [W] [MF] ([PS]: dozens)
- Physicians blamed overheated ("hot") blood and prescribed more dancing. [W] [PDR] [MF]
- The city built a stage and hired musicians; it backfired. [PDR] [PS] [MF] [W]
- By August as many as 400 were dancing. [PDR] [PS] [MF] ([W]: estimates from 50 to 400)
- Some may have died; the toll is unknown and disputed. [W] [PDR] [PS] [MF]
- The council then banned public dancing and sent dancers to a shrine of St Vitus. [W] [PDR] [PS]
- It was over by early September. [W] [PDR] [MF]
- Historian John Waller's explanation: stress-induced mass illness after famine and disease. [W] [PDR] [PS] [MF]
