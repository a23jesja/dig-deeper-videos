# Victor Lustig, the man who sold the Eiffel Tower — checked facts for Sunday's short + long video

Source: Wikipedia "Victor Lustig", read 2026-11-13. Only these may go in the script.

- Born 4 January 1890 in Hostinné, Bohemia, Austria-Hungary (now Czech Republic). "One of the most notorious con artists of his time."
  At a Dresden boarding school he learned English, French, Italian and Hungarian. Aliases: Count Lustig, Robert Miller, Robert Duval.
- Expelled on his first day of infant school for telling a teacher "Leck mich am Arsch". A 1902 school report: "retarded morally, an idiot in other respects".
- Early cons on ocean liners between France and New York: he posed as a musical producer seeking investors for a Broadway production that did not exist.
- The "Rumanian Box" (money box): a mahogany box about the size of a steamer trunk, two slots and dummy levers, said to copy any banknote —
  "the only catch being that the device needed six hours to print a copy". A genuine note was hidden inside in advance; he took the buyer to a bank
  to prove the "copy" was real, then sold the box for an exorbitant sum. A Texas sheriff paid thousands of dollars, chased him to Chicago,
  and was paid back — in counterfeit bills.
- 1925, Paris: he read a newspaper article about how expensive it was to maintain and repaint the Eiffel Tower.
  He posed as the Deputy Director-General of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs (forged government stationery),
  invited a small group of scrap metal dealers to an expensive hotel, and told them — in strict secrecy — that the government wished to sell the tower for scrap.
- The victim: André Poisson, an insecure man who wanted to rise in Parisian business circles. He paid a large bribe plus the money for the "sale" (around 70,000 francs in all).
  Lustig fled to Austria. Poisson was too ashamed and embarrassed to tell the police; the newspapers said nothing.
- Later that year Lustig went back to Paris and tried it again with other dealers. This time the police were informed, and he fled to the United States.
- Al Capone: Lustig asked him to invest $50,000 in a scheme, kept the money in a safe deposit box for two months, and returned it saying the deal had fallen through.
  Capone, impressed, gave him $5,000 (other sources: $1,000) to "tide him over".
- From 1930: counterfeiting with pharmacist William Watts and chemist Tom Shaw — thousands of dollars of fake money a month for five years ("Lustig money").
- His mistress Billy May, betrayed for a younger woman, made an anonymous phone call to the federal authorities. Arrested 10 May 1935 in New York.
  A key he would not explain opened a locker in the Times Square subway station: $51,000 in counterfeit bills and the plates.
- 1 September 1935, the day before his trial: he faked illness and climbed out of the Federal House of Detention in New York on a specially made rope.
  Recaptured 27 days later in Pittsburgh.
- Pleaded guilty. 9 December 1935: fifteen years on Alcatraz, plus five for the escape (20 years).
- Died of pneumonia on 11 March 1947 at the Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, Springfield, Missouri.
  His death certificate gave his occupation as "apprentice salesman and counterfeiter".
- "Ten Commandments for Con Men" (attributed): be a patient listener; never look bored; wait for the other person to reveal political opinions, then agree;
  the same with religion; never discuss illness; never pry; never boast; never be untidy; never get drunk.
- NOT in the article (do not use): the Hôtel de Crillon, the number of dealers, the tonnage of iron, a bed-sheet rope or window-cleaner disguise, rust.
