## Facts used

The painting
- Oil on a poplar wood panel, 77 x 53 cm. [WM] [WP] [SL] [ME]
- Begun by Leonardo in Florence around 1503. [TI] [WM]
- Leonardo took it with him to France (1516); King Francis I acquired it after his death in 1519. Napoleon never
  took it from Italy. [WM] [WP] [SL] [TI]  ("nearly four hundred years" in 1911 = 392 years.)
- It hung in the Salon Carré on four iron hooks/pegs and was not otherwise fixed to the wall. [WP] [SL]
- Before 1911 it was admired and received love letters, but was "one among many highly regarded artworks" and not
  yet known to everyone. [WM] [SL] [TI]
- The Louvre's director had said that one might as well pretend one could steal the towers of Notre-Dame. He was
  on holiday at the time of the theft. [WP] [SL]

Peruggia
- Vincenzo Peruggia, born 8 October 1881 in Dumenza, northern Italy, near Switzerland. [WP] [ME]
- A house painter / decorator; about 160 cm (5 ft 3). [WP] [ME] [SL] [TI]
- Came to Paris for work and had worked at the Louvre; he helped make the glass case for the Mona Lisa. [WP] [WM] [SL] [TI] [ME]
- He was no longer working at the Louvre at the time of the theft. [WP] [ME]
- He had been arrested before; the police had his photograph and fingerprints. [WP] [SL]

The theft
- Monday 21 August 1911, about 7 a.m.; the Louvre was closed to the public on Mondays. [WP] [ME] [SL] [TI]
- He walked in through the workers' entrance wearing the white smock that museum workmen wore. [WP] [ME]; smock also [SL]
  The overnight stay in a cupboard is rejected by [ME], absent from [WP], and only "probably / may" in [SL].
- He lifted the painting off its hooks, took it to a service staircase, and removed the glass case and the frame,
  which were found there. [WP] [SL] [ME]
- The door at the bottom was locked; he removed the doorknob; a plumber (Sauvet) who took him for a workman
  opened it. [WP] (plumber) [SL] [CR] [HH] (doorknob, Sauvet)
- He wrapped the painting in his smock and carried it under his arm. [ME] [WP] (WP rejects "under the smock")
- He took it home to his room in Paris. [WP] [SL] [TI]

Discovery and uproar
- Nobody raised the alarm on Monday: the guards assumed the museum photographers had it. [ME] [WM] [SL] [TI]
- Tuesday 22 August: the painter Louis Béroud came to paint it and found the hooks; the alarm was raised at about
  11 a.m., more than 24 hours after the theft. [WM] [SL] [ME]
- The Louvre was closed for about a week. [WM] [SL]  (Time says nine days.)
- About sixty policemen / detectives worked on it. [WP] [SP] [CR]
- A thumbprint was found. [WP] [SL]  (WP: on the glass; Slate: on the wall — the script does not say where.)
- Ships and trains were searched and the borders watched. [TI] [WP]
- Rewards were offered. [WP] [TI] [SP] [MF]  (The amount of 25,000 francs from the Friends of the Louvre has only [SP]: not used.)
- Front-page news around the world. [SL] [TI]
- The Louvre's director, Théophile Homolle, was forced to resign. [SP] [TC]
- When the Louvre reopened people queued to see the empty wall; thousands came; Franz Kafka, travelling with
  Max Brod, was among them. [SL] [TI] [CR] [HH] [TC]

The wrong suspects
- Géry Pieret, once Apollinaire's secretary, wrote to a Paris newspaper about a week after the theft saying he had
  stolen statuettes from the Louvre. [AN] [TI] [SL]
- Two of the stolen Iberian statuettes had ended up with Picasso. [TI] [AN] [SL]
- The statuettes were handed back. [AN] [SL]
- Apollinaire was arrested on 7 September 1911 and held for several days in prison (7 to 11 September, or "a week"). [AN] [SL] [SP] [CR] [HH]
- Picasso was questioned before the judge, was terrified, and denied knowing Apollinaire. [SL] [MF] [CR]  (Fear of deportation: one source, not used.)
- Both were released; there was no evidence. [WP] [WM] [AN] [SL] [TI]

Two years in hiding
- A detective visited Peruggia's room, questioned him, believed him and did not find the painting. [WP] [SL] [SP] [TC]
- The police had his fingerprints but never matched them to the print. [WP] [SL]
- The painting lay first on a table under a cloth, then in a wooden trunk/crate with a false bottom, for more than
  two years. [ME] [SL] (table) · [ME] [WP] [TC] [CR] (trunk, false bottom)
- The Louvre hung a portrait by Raphael in the empty space. [SL] [s] "The Crimes of Paris"  **(weakest fact in the script: one source read, one only seen in a search summary; cut the line if in doubt)**

Florence
- Late November 1913: the Florentine dealer Alfredo Geri received a letter from Paris signed "Leonardo". [SL] [WP] [HH]
- Peruggia asked for 500,000 lire. [SL] [AN]
- Geri brought in Giovanni Poggi, director of the Uffizi; the painting was taken to the Uffizi and found genuine;
  they agreed to his price and then informed the police. [WP] [SL] [TI] [HH]
- He stayed at the Hotel Tripoli-Italia and was arrested there in December 1913 (11 or 12 December). [SL] [WP] [TC] [SP]  ([TC] calls it "Hotel Tripoli, now Hotel Gioconda".)

Motive, trial
- He said he acted as a patriot, to return what Napoleon had stolen. [WP] [SL] [TI] [AN]
- His letters to his family show he hoped for money ("I will make my fortune", 22 December 1911). [WP] [ME] [AN]
- The Valfierno / Chaudron "six forgeries" story comes only from Karl Decker's article in the Saturday Evening Post,
  25 June 1932; there is no other evidence. [WP] [WM] [ME] [SL] [TI]
- He was hailed as a patriot by many in Italy. [WP] [WM] [TI] [SL]
- Tried in Florence in June 1914: one year and fifteen days, reduced to about seven months; released for time
  served. [WP] [SL] [SP] [TC] [HH]  ([CR] says about eight months.)

After
- The painting was shown in Italy (the Uffizi for over two weeks) and returned to the Louvre on 4 January 1914. [WM] [WP] [SL] [TI]
- The theft and its publicity made it one of the best-known pictures in the world. [WP] [WM] [SL] [TI]
- Peruggia served in the Italian army in the First World War, returned to France, and died on 8 October 1925, his
  44th birthday, in Saint-Maur-des-Fossés. [WP] [ME] [SL]
- His death went unreported; obituaries appeared only in 1947, when another man with (or using) his name died in
  Haute-Savoie. [WP] [SL] [ME]

## Conflicts between sources (how the script handles them)
- Cupboard overnight or walked in on Monday: the script says "by his own account, he simply walked in".
- Under the smock or wrapped in it: wrapped, under his arm.
- Where the print was, and why it was not matched (right-hand prints only, or his prints never compared): the
  script only says the match was never made.
- Louvre closed a week / nine days: "a week".
- Sentence served: seven months [WP] [SL]; Wikipedia's Mona Lisa article says six.
- Arrest 11 or 12 December 1913: "in December".
- 1947: "another Vincenzo Peruggia" [WP] [SL] or an imposter using his name [ME]: "another man using his name".
- Peruggia's trade: carpenter in some accounts; police records say painter [ME].
