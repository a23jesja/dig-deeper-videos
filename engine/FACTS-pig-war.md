# The Pig War (1859) — checked facts for Saturday's short + long video

Source: Wikipedia "Pig War (1859)", read 2026-11-13. Only these may go in the script.

- San Juan Islands, between Vancouver Island (Britain) and the Washington Territory (US). The Oregon Treaty of 15 June 1846 put the border
  in "the middle of the channel" — but there were two channels: Haro Strait (west of the islands) and Rosario Strait (east). Both countries claimed the islands.
- 15 June 1859 (exactly 13 years after the treaty): Lyman Cutlar, an American farmer from Kentucky, found a pig rooting in his garden and
  eating his tubers (potatoes). It was not the first time. He shot and killed the pig.
- The pig belonged to an Irishman, Charles Griffin, who ran the Hudson's Bay Company's sheep ranch (Belle Vue sheep farm) on the island; he let his pigs roam freely.
- Cutlar offered $10. Griffin demanded $100. Dialogue (the article calls it "likely apocryphal" — say "the story goes"):
  Cutlar: "It was eating my potatoes." Griffin: "It is up to you to keep your potatoes out of my pig."
- British authorities threatened to arrest Cutlar; the American settlers (25-29 of them by mid-1859) asked for military protection.
- US Brigadier General William S. Harney sent Captain George Pickett with 66 soldiers of the 9th Infantry.
  Pickett: "We'll make a Bunker Hill of it." (Pickett later led Pickett's Charge at Gettysburg, 1863.)
- The British sent three warships under Captain Geoffrey Hornby. Governor James Douglas ordered Hornby to dislodge the Americans, avoiding armed conflict if possible.
- By 10 August 1859: 461 Americans with 14 cannons (Colonel Silas Casey) faced five British warships with 70 guns and 2,140 men.
- Rear Admiral Robert L. Baynes arrived and would not escalate into a war between two great nations "over a squabble about a pig".
- September 1859: President James Buchanan sent General Winfield Scott to negotiate. Both sides agreed to a joint military occupation,
  no more than 100 men each: British camp at the north end, American camp at the south end.
- That lasted 12 years. The two camps visited each other to celebrate national holidays and held athletic competitions.
  (Park rangers say the biggest threat to peace was the large amount of alcohol available.)
- The question went to arbitration by the German Emperor, Wilhelm I. On 21 October 1872 the decision went to the United States (Haro Strait border).
  The Royal Marines left on 25 November 1872; the Americans by July 1874.
- No human casualties on either side — only the pig. ("Bloodless war".)
- Today: San Juan Island National Historical Park. The Union Jack still flies over the old British camp, raised and lowered daily by American park rangers.
- NOT in the article (do not use): the pig's breed/colour/size, orders not to fire first, soldiers trading insults, ship names other than HMS Satellite.
