# The Great Emu War (short, 55 s)

## Facts (checked 2026-11-12 against en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu_War and its cited sources)
- 2 November - 10 December 1932, Campion district, Western Australia; about 20,000 emus (estimate).
- Major G. P. W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery) with two soldiers (Sgt S. McMurray, Gunner J. O'Halloran),
  two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds.
- 2 Nov: the birds were out of range; herded toward an ambush they "split into small groups and ran".
- 4 Nov: ambush near a dam, more than 1,000 emus approached; the gun jammed after 12 birds were killed, the rest scattered.
- A gun was mounted on a truck: the truck could not gain on the birds and the ride was so rough the gunner could not fire a shot.
- By 8 Nov, six days after the first engagement, 2,500 rounds had been fired; the force was withdrawn that day.
- Second attempt from 13 Nov; recalled 10 Dec. Meredith's own report: 986 emus killed with 9,860 rounds
  (disputed by the farmers and by historians, so the script says "by the major's count").

## Narration (Sir Digsworth, Kokoro bm_george, speed 1.2)
1. In 1932, Australia sent its army to war against twenty thousand emus.
2. The emus won.
3. Twenty thousand hungry emus had marched onto the wheat farms of Western Australia, eating everything in sight.
4. So the army sent one major, two soldiers, two machine guns and ten thousand bullets.
5. Easy, right?
6. But when the guns opened fire, the emus split into small groups and ran in every direction.
7. So the major set an ambush at a dam.
8. A thousand emus walked straight towards the gun,
9. and it jammed.
10. Next, he put a gun on a truck.
11. But the truck couldn't catch the birds, and the ride was so bumpy the gunner never fired a shot.
12. After six days and two and a half thousand bullets, the army went home.
13. They came back for a second try.
14. Final score, by the major's count: 986 emus, for nearly ten thousand bullets.
15. And the emus?
16. They're still there.
