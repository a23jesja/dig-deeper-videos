## Facts (checked 2026-11-16; pages opened and quoted; two source families for every spoken claim)
Sources: [P] Pliny the Younger, letters 6.16 and 6.20 to Tacitus (Project Gutenberg #2811, letters LXV and LXVI);
[W] Wikipedia "Eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD", "Pompeii", "Herculaneum", "House of the Tragic Poet",
"Giuseppe Fiorelli"; [SM] Smithsonian Magazine "Resurrecting Pompeii"; [BBC] BBC History "Pompeii: Portents of
Disaster"; [LS] Live Science "Mount Vesuvius & Pompeii"; [WHE] World History Encyclopedia "Pompeii"; [NG] National
Geographic "Pompeii"; [MWA] Museum of Western Australia / Athena Review (the dog cast, via search summary).
- Year 79. The day is NOT said: August 24 is the traditional date, an inscription found in 2018 points to October. [W] [WHE]
- About 11,000 people. [W] "11,000 to 11,500"; [WHE] "10-12,000".
- Bakeries and bread: [W] "at least 31 bakeries"; [SM] "An oven in an excavated bakery was found to contain 81
  carbonized loaves". The numbers 31 and 81 have one family each, so the film says only "bread in the oven".
- "Beware of the dog": [W] floor mosaic of a chained dog with "CAVE CANEM" at the House of the Tragic Poet; History.com
  and World History Encyclopedia list the same mosaic (search result, not opened).
- Earth tremors for days, thought normal: [P] "for many days before a trembling of the earth, which did not alarm us
  much, as this is quite an ordinary occurrence in Campania"; [BBC] same passage discussed.
- It began around midday: [P] "about one in the afternoon"; [WHE] "at midday"; [LS] "shortly after noon".
- The cloud like a pine tree: [P] "likening it to that of a pine tree, for it shot up to a great height in the form of
  a very tall trunk, which spread itself out at the top into a sort of branches"; [SM] [LS].
- Nobody was prepared: [BBC] "it caught the local population utterly unprepared"; [SM] "most people would not have
  known that Vesuvius was a volcano".
- People carried coins and jewellery: [SM] "a middle-aged man carrying gold jewelry, a sack of coins"; [W] [BBC].
- Stones (pumice) fell: [P] "calcined stones and cinders, though light indeed, yet fell in large showers"; [W] [SM] [BBC].
- Dark in daytime: [P] "It was now day everywhere else, but there a deeper darkness prevailed than in the thickest
  night"; [LS] "In Pompeii, ash blocked the sun by 1 p.m."; [NG] quotes Pliny's "as if the lamp had been put out".
- Pillows on heads: [P] "having pillows tied upon their heads with napkins" (Pliny the Elder's party at Stabiae, a
  few kilometres down the coast from Pompeii, hence "down the coast"). Torches: [P] "torches and other lights".
- Nearly three metres of pumice; roofs broke: [W] "a 2.8 m layer"; [BBC] "the fall of 3m (10ft) of pumice"; roofs [W] [SM].
- Shouting, children and parents calling: [P] "the shrieks of women, the screams of children, and the shouts of men;
  some calling for their children, others for their parents"; quoted by [SM].
- The sea too rough to leave: [P] "found the waves still running extremely high" (the shore near Stabiae). Shown with
  a tag naming Pliny, because it is his shore, not Pompeii's harbour.
- The admiral: Pliny the Elder commanded the fleet at Misenum, sailed to rescue people and died on the shore. [P] [SM] [BBC]
- The mountain glowing at night: [P] "broad flames shone out in several places from Mount Vesuvius"; [W].
- Most got away, about 2,000 stayed: [SM] "An estimated 80 percent of Pompeii's residents likely fled ... but more than
  2,000 stayed behind, huddled inside buildings"; [W] "allowing most inhabitants to escape"; [LS] "Around 2,000".
- The glowing cloud next morning: [LS] "About 6:30 a.m. on the following morning, a glowing cloud of volcanic gases and
  debris rolled down Vesuvius' slopes and enveloped the city"; [W] "In the early morning ... incinerating or
  suffocating the remaining population", hot enough "to kill people in a fraction of a second"; [SM] "Daybreak".
- Buried under metres of ash and pumice: [W] "4 to 6 m"; [SM] "ten feet". The depth differs, so no number is said.
- Digging began in 1748: [W]; [NG] "discovered by a surveying engineer in 1748". 1748 - 79 = 1,669 years.
- Plaster casts from hollows in the ash, 1863, Giuseppe Fiorelli: [W]; [SM] "In 1863 ... Giuseppe Fiorelli noticed four
  cavities in the hardened layer ... By filling the holes with plaster".
- A chained guard dog among the casts: [W] "the plaster cast of a chained dog killed in Pompeii"; [MWA] (page opened
  2026-11-16) "Guard dog. Material: Cast. Location: House of Orpheus, Pompeii. This dog was left chained to a post to
  guard the House of Orpheus when the occupants fled." The year it was found (1874) comes from a search summary only
  (the Athena Review page did not open): do NOT show "FOUND 1874" in Part 3 unless a second opened source gives it.
- More than a thousand bodies found: [W] "approximately 1,150"; Fox Weather 2024 (opened) "Park officials said the
  remains of more than 1,000 victims of the eruption have been discovered so far"; Popular Archaeology (opened)
  "Approximately 1,150+ human remains have been found or excavated in Pompeii to date".
- A third still unexcavated: [WHE] "one third remains unexcavated"; [W] "Two-thirds of the city has been excavated".
NOT said, because the sources disagree or only one family has it: the day and month; the height of the cloud (33 km
or 43 km); the speed and temperature of the glowing cloud; the number of bakeries and loaves; how many hours the
stones fell; the total number of dead; that the people did not know Vesuvius was a volcano.
Dramatised (each film says so in its second line): Sir Digsworth being there, the baker, soldier, merchant, boy and
dog as people, Sir freeing the dog, his walk out of the town.
