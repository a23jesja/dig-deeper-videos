## Facts (checked 2026-11-16; pages opened and quoted; two source families for every spoken claim)
Sources: [S] Wikipedia "Sinking of the Titanic"; [US] US Senate inquiry report 1912 (titanicinquiry.org, AmInqRep03/06/07);
[NG] National Geographic "Titanic". Earlier checks (2026-11-14, FACTS-titanic.md): [T] Wikipedia "Titanic", [C] Wikipedia
"RMS Carpathia", [NA] UK National Archives.
- Distress rockets were fired, one after another. [US] "The Titanic fired distress rockets"; "distress rockets were fired at
  frequent intervals". [S] "rockets were fired, one going up every few minutes".
- The forward part went under first (she sank by the bow). [US] "when the forward part of the ship was submerged". [S] [NG].
- She disappeared at about 2:20 a.m. on 15 April 1912. [NG] "At 2:20 a.m. on April 15, 1912, the 'unsinkable' R.M.S. Titanic
  disappeared beneath the waves". [S] time 23:40-02:20.
- An overturned collapsible lifeboat served as a raft for about thirty men. [US] "in its overturned condition served as a life
  raft for about 30 people, including Second Officer Lightoller". [S] "some 35 men were clinging precariously to the upturned hull".
- The Carpathia arrived at about 4 a.m. and picked up the survivors. [S] [C] [NG] (2026-11-14); [US] "picked up by the Carpathia".
- About 700 were saved (706 [US], 710 [S]); about 1,500 died ([NG] "1,500 souls"; [S] 1,490-1,517).
- About 1,500 were still on board when the boats had gone (about 2,200 aboard minus about 700 in the boats; used in Part 2).
NOT said, because the sources disagree or only one family has it: the ship breaking in two ([US] says she "sank intact"),
the lights going out and the stern rising high ([S] only), the band playing to the end ([S] has witnesses who say it stopped
earlier), the water temperature. Do not show the ship breaking in two.
Dramatised (the film says so in line 2): Sir Digsworth being on board, swimming to the overturned boat, being pulled up.

## Shots (every shot starts 1-3 frames before its word; a wide view lasts 2 s at most; the picture shows what is said)
1. hook, "The Titanic is sinking": FIRST FRAME = the sea running up the tilted deck toward Sir Digsworth, who stands big
   (at least half the picture height) on the planks, already stepping back; the deck is tilted (camera roll 7-10 degrees,
   water line climbing the planks); a rocket is already climbing. Hook text "THE TITANIC IS SINKING" on screen from frame 0
   in a band where no figure and no effect is. On "And I am still on board": he looks at the lens, shocked.
2. truth: close-up of Sir Digsworth talking (mouth follows the voice), calm face; small tag "THE EVENTS ARE TRUE / my part is
   dramatised" clear of his face and of the captions.
3. recap, "The lifeboats are gone": the empty davits with their ropes hanging, lifeboats (3D, with seated people) small on
   the dark water rowing away. Then "About 1,500 of us are left": the deck packed with people, row behind row down the deck
   (at least 60 figures visible, the far ones small; same few drawings repeated), Sir Digsworth in front, big.
4. rockets: low on the deck: a rocket climbs and bursts in a star, then a second one; Sir Digsworth and the officer LOOK UP
   at it (heads tilted back, eyes up) when it bursts; their shadows fall away from the burst, soft.
5. bow, "The bow goes under": looking down the tilted deck toward the bow: the water has covered the forward deck and climbs;
   on "The sea comes up the deck": Sir Digsworth, the officer and the gentleman climb up the slope away from it (side view,
   walking, feet planted), a deck bench slides down past them.
6. gone, "At 2:20 in the morning": from a lifeboat on the water (2 s at most): the stern of the ship, low, sliding under
   (the whole ship in one piece), people in the boat's bow in the foreground looking at it. On "the Titanic is gone": only
   the dark sea, stars, floating deck chairs and planks; a tag "2:20 A.M. · 15 APRIL 1912".
7. boat, "I swim for an overturned lifeboat": Sir Digsworth in the water (only head, shoulders and arms above it, cap on,
   pipe in), swimming toward the upturned hull. On "About thirty men cling to it": the upturned boat with thirty men on it
   (count them: thirty figures standing and kneeling in rows on the hull); the officer reaches down and pulls him up.
8. dawn, "At about four, a ship arrives": first light on the horizon (dusk-coloured sky, calmer water), the Carpathia (a
   one-funnel liner, 3D) coming toward the lifeboats; the men on the hull look toward her and wave. Tag "THE CARPATHIA · ABOUT 4 A.M."
9. count: on the Carpathia's deck at dawn, survivors in blankets in rows; a board with two lines appearing on their words:
   "ABOUT 700 SAVED" and "ABOUT 1,500 LOST". Sir Digsworth in a blanket reads it; nobody smiles.
10. end, "I survived the Titanic. Most did not.": close-up of Sir Digsworth at the rail in the dawn light, looking at the
   lens, then turning his eyes to the empty sea. Hold 0.8 s after the last word.
Sound: the voice at -16 LUFS, the house score low under it (sad low strings, a timpani on each rocket burst and on "gone",
a light theme at dawn), sea, rocket whoosh and bang, creaking, splashes. Mix -14 LUFS.
