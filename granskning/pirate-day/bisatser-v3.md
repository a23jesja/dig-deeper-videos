# Pirate day, voice script v3 against the clause rule (18 Nov)

The rule (voice.md, "How I talk", Jesper 18 November) says there must be no inserted clauses, because the voice does not pause clearly at commas. Each listed sentence is split into short sentences.

I checked every sentence in `manus-v3-rosttext.md`. Eight must be split. The facts and the [SOURCE]/[OWN] marks are unchanged: only the sentence boundaries move.

| Time | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| 1:44 | Roberts' rules say every man keeps his musket, pistols and cutlass clean and ready, and men competed to own the finest pistols, worn over the shoulder on coloured ribbons. | Roberts' rules say every man keeps his musket, pistols and cutlass clean and ready. Men competed to own the finest pistols. They wore them over the shoulder, on coloured ribbons. |
| 1:58 | On Howell Davis's ship, where Roberts learned the trade, the rules promised that whoever spotted a sail first, if she was taken, would get the best pair of pistols on board. | Roberts learned the trade on Howell Davis's ship. Its rules made a promise. Whoever spotted a sail first would get the best pair of pistols on board, if she was taken. |
| 3:02 | Unless supplies ran short (which, the book admits, was no uncommon thing). | Unless supplies ran short. The book admits that was no uncommon thing. |
| 3:13 | So he went and took a ship with plenty of liquor on board, and then, he wrote, all things went well. | So he went and took a ship with plenty of liquor on board. Then he wrote that all things went well. |
| 3:36 | Bartholomew Roberts, the man who wrote your rules, preferred tea. | Bartholomew Roberts wrote your rules. He preferred tea. |
| 5:20 | Pirates often flew false colours, a friendly flag, so their target would let them come close. | Pirates often flew false colours. A friendly flag let them come close to their target. |
| 6:05 | Gold and silver, the crew has voted, never go back. | The crew has voted on gold and silver. They never go back. |
| 6:30 | And your name is called from a list, in turn, for a new set of clothes from the prize. | Names are called from a list, one by one. When yours comes, you get a new set of clothes from the prize. |

Kept as they are: lists, and clauses at the end of a sentence, which the voice reads in order. For example:
- 0:03 "...came over the horizon, your captain gave up without a fight, and now the pirates want you";
- 0:30 "...voted out, if he's a coward or a bully";
- 5:47 "...could cost lives, on both sides".

Nothing is re-recorded until Jesper approves the list.
