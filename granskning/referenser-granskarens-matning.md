## What I measured across all five

- **Figure height:** usually 60–100 % of the frame height. Close-ups crop the figure at the chest, or the head
  fills half the width or more. Wide shots with small figures last about 1 s each and are crowds or a map, never
  two people talking.
- **The camera is close and mostly level with the faces.**
  - The castle stairs, the bridge and the warship use over-the-shoulder shots: the back of a head or a shoulder in
    the foreground, sharp or blurred, with the other figure acting behind it.
- **Something new about every 0.5–1.5 s,** counting both a new shot and an action inside a shot:
  - a hammer swing, the paint roller going up and down, a plane banking, a crowd running at the lens;
  - a king tripping, a face changing colour, scissors cutting a braid.
  - No shot holds a figure still with only its eyes moving.
- **Faces carry the joke:** big changes within one shot.
  - The calm builder's grin turns to a toothy smirk.
  - The king goes white, then red, then purple with flames and an anger mark.
  - The soldier's wink turns to a sweat drop, then to shock.
  - Mouths open wide when a character speaks.
- **Bodies are simple:** stick or tube limbs, simple hands (round or a white glove), simple walks. The animation
  is not complex; it is constant, clear and readable.
- **Props tell the facts:**
  - a folding rule held up to the step;
  - a flip counter with a sign;
  - model ships on a map;
  - a date carved in a stone;
  - a call-up paper with a seal, held by a hand.
  - **Hand-and-object close-ups do occur** (the call-up paper, the scissors), but each lasts at most 1.5 s, and a
    face shot comes next.
- **Light and colour:** one theme per scene, such as warm sun on stone, flat grey daylight on riveted steel,
  low sun with glare, or a purple-red battlefield. Medium shots have a blurred background (only in the 3D ones).
- **Captions:** big, word by word, in the middle of the lower half, plus a small line at the top in some of them.
  We do not copy their caption style; ours follows DAILY-RULES.

## What the fifth reference (the soldier) teaches

It is flat 2D, like our Knopp style, and still reaches the level. What makes the difference is not 3D but these
four things:
- the figures fill the frame;
- every shot has an action;
- the faces change strongly;
- the props are drawn large.

Our Eiffel v10 and v11 failed on exactly these, plus the extra hands. The 3D step can still fail the same way if
the figures stand still in a beautiful set.

## How the review uses this

For each shot in a STOPP, the build session writes the closest reference shot, from `referenser-3d.md` or from
this note. A shot fails if any of these holds:
- (a) a figure is under about 45 % of the height in a shot with two people talking;
- (b) more than 1.5 s passes with nothing new (a camera drift alone does not count);
- (c) a face does not change while the voice tells something that would move it;
- (d) the reviewer cannot point to a reference shot at the same level.
