## Description
Did gladiators really fight to the death? Not usually. About nine times in ten, a gladiator lived to fight another day. Each one was trained, fed and doctored, and that cost a great deal. The last thing the school's owner wanted was a dead one. There were rules, and a referee. A beaten man raised a finger to give up. And most of the time, he was spared. So no: most fights ended with both men walking out.

"About nine times in ten" is National Geographic's estimate: "For every 10 gladiators who entered the ring, nine probably lived to fight another day" (Andrew Curry, 2021). In the same article the art historian Katherine Welch (New York University) says: "Out of 10 pairs, there would be one death, possibly two." Both give the estimate for Roman gladiators in general, without a period or a place, and the film shows an arena with no name and no year. Some fights did end in death. That most fights ended with both men walking out is R. R. R. Smith's (University of Oxford) reading in Archaeology Magazine. The arena doctor is Galen, who wrote that no gladiators died in the five years he cared for them (Archaeology Magazine).

The scenes are dramatised. "Do you know what you cost me?" and "Relax. They'll probably be fine." are our own lines, not quotes.
