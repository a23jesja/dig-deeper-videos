## Description
Did Nero fiddle while Rome burned? Not so fast. When the fire began, says Tacitus, Nero was not even in Rome: he was at Antium, the modern Anzio, about 50 km from Rome. He came back and opened his own gardens to the homeless. The fire raged for six days, and a rumour spread: while Rome burned, Nero sang of the fall of Troy. Sang, perhaps. Fiddled, never. The fiddle came about a thousand years later. His instrument was the cithara, a kind of lyre with seven strings. So no fiddle. Maybe a lyre. And he probably wasn't even there.

The scenes are dramatised, and the lines in the speech bubbles are our own, not quotes. The Roman writers disagree: Tacitus calls the singing a rumour, Suetonius and Cassius Dio state it as fact, and they do not agree on who started the fire. This video does not say who did.
