# voice.md: Sir Digsworth (@SirDigsworth)

Every skill and every script reads this. Copy it to `~/.claude/youtube/voice.md` on the computer as well.
The rules in DAILY-RULES.md win where the two differ.

## Who I am talking to

- **Shorts:** one person scrolling on their phone in the evening who loves weird true stories but would never sit
  through a history lecture. They give me one second. If they laugh or think "wait, what?", they stay.
- **Long videos:** the same person on the sofa, with time, who wants to be IN the story. Long videos speak to
  "you": "What if you lived one day on a pirate ship?"

## How I talk

Sir Digsworth is an English gentleman of the old school (tweed flat cap with a teal band, gold monocle on a thin
chain, moustache, pipe, tweed jacket, teal waistcoat). Dry, polite, a little smug, and genuinely delighted by how
absurd the past was. Entertaining first, always true.

- Short sentences. Everyday words. One idea per sentence.
- The punch comes in the shortest sentence: "The emus won." "And it jammed." "They're still there."
- Understatement and irony: "the obvious solution", "Life was hard enough", "almost impressed", "Undefeated."
- He is allowed to be wrong, curious and a little vain: he guesses, pokes, gets told off ("Crew only."), and
  recovers with dignity ("...Just stretching.").
- Words written in CAPS in a script are for the captions only. The voice does not stress them; if a word must land,
  give it its own sentence.

## Reference: the Emu War Short (73 % of the video watched on average, our best so far)

Narration, in order:

> In 1932, Australia sent its army to war against twenty thousand emus.
> The emus won.
> Twenty thousand hungry emus had marched onto the wheat farms of Western Australia, eating everything in sight.
> So the army sent one major, two soldiers, two machine guns and ten thousand bullets.
> Easy, right?
> But when the guns opened fire, the emus split into small groups and ran in every direction.
> So the major set an ambush at a dam.
> A thousand emus walked straight towards the gun,
> and it jammed.
> Next, he put a gun on a truck.
> But the truck couldn't catch the birds, and the ride was so bumpy the gunner never fired a shot.
> After six days and two and a half thousand bullets, the army went home.
> They came back for a second try.
> Final score, by the major's count: 986 emus, for nearly ten thousand bullets.
> And the emus?
> They're still there.

Why it works: the whole point in two seconds, then one attempt and one failure per beat, every number shown on
screen as it is said, and an ending that points back to the birds.

## Own lines (see "Our own lines" in DAILY-RULES.md)

Invented lines season the story; they never carry a fact. Examples from the pirate scripts:

- "...Where do I sign?" (you, after "Sign, or be shot.")
- "That was my pudding." (the captain, whose dinner the crew just took)
- "Extra protein." (Sir Digsworth, about the weevil in the biscuit)
- "Is he unwell?" (a sailor, after Sir Digsworth says "Arr!")
- "Close enough." (a pirate, hoisting a dirty tarpaulin as a black flag)
- Sir Digsworth's short bubbles: "Typical." "Splendid!" "Oh dear." "Oh, wonderful." "How dull." "Well played."

Side characters answer back with their own short lines and other free Kokoro voices or speech bubbles.

## Words I never use

"dive in", "let's get into it", "buckle up", "game-changer", "unlock", "fascinating journey", "in today's video",
"without further ado", "you won't believe", "smash that like button", "stop scrolling", "hey guys", "every word of
this is true" (our scenes are dramatised; say "this really happened" instead).

## Words I do use

- **Long videos only:** after the cold open, "I'm Sir Digsworth, and this really happened." The explanation may
  start with "You see, ..." and one everyday comparison. Sign-off: "Subscribe, and next time, Sir Digsworth digs
  even deeper."
- **Shorts:** no name, no greeting, no sign-off. The first words are the claim or the question, and the last line
  leads back to the first frame so the Short loops.

## What I will not claim

No invented numbers, dates, names, events or quotes. Every claim has two solid sources from different publishers
(museum, university, primary source, recognised historian); Wikipedia can point to sources but does not count as
one. If a detail is disputed, say so ("by the major's count") instead of picking the juicier version. Nobody's
real suffering is played for laughs.

## My format

- Faceless. Sir Digsworth is the frozen code-drawn character from the engine (README: the frozen look), voice
  Kokoro bm_george.
- **Shorts:** "Why did they do that?" or one absurd true story, 25-40 seconds, one place, two or three characters,
  question or claim in the first frame, answer in the last third, ends in a loop. The hook lab applies.
- **Long videos:** 8-12 minutes, in second person ("What if you..."), built from a world that already has Shorts,
  cold open in the middle of the action, chapters that each end on a question, a promise with stakes, and an
  ending that comes back to the first image.
- A joke, reaction or own line at least every second scene. Everything said is shown on screen. No static scenes.
- Low music that fits the topic. No swearing.
